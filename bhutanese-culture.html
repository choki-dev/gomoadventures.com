Bhutanese Culture

Living traditions of the Land of the Thunder Dragon

Kuzuzangpo la

“Hello” in Dzongkha

In Bhutan, tradition is not kept behind glass — it is lived every day.

From the prayer flags strung across mountain passes to the national dress worn in offices and schools, Bhutanese culture is part of everyday life. Shaped by Buddhism and centuries of relative isolation, it remains one of the most distinctive cultures in the Himalayas — and one our guides are proud to share.

01

Faith

Buddhist Faith

Vajrayana Buddhism shapes daily life in Bhutan. Prayer flags, prayer wheels and chortens mark the landscape, and monasteries remain at the heart of every community.

02

Celebration

Tshechu Festivals

Held in dzongs and temples across the country, tshechus bring whole communities together to watch sacred masked dances performed by monks and laymen.

03

Dress

Gho & Kira

Men wear the gho, a knee-length robe, and women the ankle-length kira. Both are worn every day in schools, offices and dzongs.

04

Architecture

The Dzongs

Bhutan's great fortress-monasteries house both monastic communities and district administration, and set the style for the country's traditional buildings.

Codes & Crafts

Driglam Namzha

Bhutan's traditional code of etiquette guides how people dress, greet and behave, especially at dzongs, temples and formal occasions.

Zorig Chusum

The thirteen traditional arts and crafts — including painting, sculpture, weaving and woodcarving — are still taught and practised today.

A culture to be experienced, not just observed.
